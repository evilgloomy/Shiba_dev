Front desk · not a clinic chart

Arrive, and the room already knows your name.

Haven is a fictional private arrival. The work is check-in, language, and a waiting room — never a symptom, never a plan of care.

Book a visit Open the desk

The olive tree is not a metaphor for outcomes. It is just the lobby.

When you arrive

Four quiet steps. Then a chair.

Reception moves a name from the check-in queue into the waiting room, calls a room, and marks the visit seen. That is the entire operation.

Language

English, 繁體中文, or 粵語. The desk filters by it. Nobody is asked to repeat themselves in the wrong one.

Identity, and anything you were told to carry. The reason for visiting is free text. It is not sorted into a medical category.

What to bring

Three things. None of them are a symptom list.

A name that matches

Photo identification, so the queue and the person are the same.

Paper you were given

A referral letter or envelope if someone handed you one. Reception files it. Reception does not interpret it.

How to reach you

A phone number for the time window you chose. Not a history.

The lobby

Stone, sage, and a list with names on it.

Haven is deliberately not the lavender clinic down the hall. There is no practitioner grid and no programme of care. There is a desk, a language, and a chair with your name.

If you are looking for medical advice, this page will not give it. The assistant refuses. The form does not sort what you write.
